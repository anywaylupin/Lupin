# The chronicle of Fusang

The smoke of Yiwan has not cleared from the Ring.

The burnt hideouts are still smouldering, and at the end of every shift the Kuafu stand on their platforms and watch the smoke rise.

Fusang has begun to count its years again, from this.

Before the new count covers the old one, this chronicle sets the old one down whole: ten thousand years, from the death of a star to the first hideout Xihe ever found.

It takes no side.

It knows what each side hid from the others, and it will say so.

## Before the star died

There was a world like Earth, in another galaxy, and for a long time it was fortunate.

It turned around a living star, and its people climbed further than Earth has climbed today.

Stations circled it in orbit.

Fusion grids fed its cities.

Minds that had never been born thought beside its people, and a single planetary network bound the whole world into one murmuring voice.

It was a world that had stopped expecting its sky to do anything but shine.

The world has no name yet.

This chronicle does not give it one.

What it keeps from that age is a way of counting: this chronicle counts every year from the hour the star died.

## Years 0 to 200: Riluo, the Sunset

Fusang calls the age that began with its star's death Riluo (日落), the Sunset, as if it had only been an evening.

It was not an evening.

The star died in a burst of radiation, and the burst reached the world before anything else did.

Every grid died at once.

Everything that ran on them went quiet in the same breath, the stations and the thinking machines and the network, and a civilisation that had never stopped talking to itself fell silent.

Nothing that came afterwards on Fusang was as large as that silence.

Every war in this chronicle happens in the quiet it left.

The star did not vanish.

It shrank into a remnant, cold and dim, and Fusang calls it Xukai.

The world still circles it, as if it had not noticed that the fire went out.

Ten thousand years later Xukai still hangs in the sky like a coin on a closed eye, and gives almost nothing back.

### Tianhuo, the Sky Fire

Then the sky caught fire.

Fusang calls it Tianhuo (天火), the Sky Fire: the storm of meteors that came after the burst.

The meteors struck only one half of the world, the hemisphere that faced the dead star, and that half became the Dayside.

The other half, the Nightside, received nothing at all, and froze.

On the Dayside, each continent caught its own share of the fire, its own seeds, and in time built its own civilisation around them.

Fusang is one of those continents, the Asia of that world.

The others still talk to one another across the distance, and some of them are as powerful as Xihe is today.

Their stories come later.

What fell, Fusang calls Yunjin (陨金), fallen gold.

It came in three parts, and the whole history of the continent is the history of who held which.

At its heart lay the Yin (阴), the seeds: Yunjin's burning core, ten small suns.

A whole Yin seed never runs down.

Around the seeds lay the common Yunjin, the bulk of the meteors, the metal from which every advanced thing on Fusang would one day be made: the dome and the tower, the carriages and the machines.

And scattered through it lay the Yang (阳), the antiseed, which seemed to do nothing at all.

The Yang is the seed's counterpart.

In any normal process it is inert, and it is poison.

It answers to one thing only, the raw and unfiltered light of a Yin seed.

When Yin and Yang meet they consume each other, and leave behind a burnt husk and a new, weaker Yin.

No one on Fusang would see it happen until Shejiu, nearly three thousand years later.

### Hanye, the Cold Night

Ten small suns lay burning in a world that had lost its own.

Fusang calls the years that followed Hanye (寒夜), the Cold Night, and they belonged to whoever could reach a seed and keep it.

Ten armies and corporations reached them.

Each seized one seed and held it, and holding it turned each of them into something more lasting than an army and harder than a company: a house.

They named themselves after the ten Heavenly Stems: Jia, Yi, Bing, Ding, Wu, Ji, Geng, Xin, Ren and Gui.

There was an eleventh house, Yao (尧), and it held no seed.

It held people instead, more of them than any of the ten.

Yao understood exactly what that was worth: a great deal against any one house, and nothing at all against the ten together.

That arithmetic would decide nearly every choice Yao made for the next ten thousand years.

## Year 200: The Xihe Covenant

In the year 200, as the Sunset ended, the ten houses stopped circling one another and allied.

They called the alliance Xihe.

From then on ten suns stood under one name, and nothing on Fusang could stand against them.

Yao refused to join.

It was not pride, and it was not principle.

Yao has only ever cared about two things, its own benefit and the safety of its own people, and in the year 200 it saw no need to sign away either.

So it stood outside the covenant, the most populous house on Fusang and the only one without a sun, and waited to see what the ten would build.

## Years 200 to 600: The building of Shangri-La

For four hundred years Xihe built.

It raised a dome, and under the dome a city, Shangri-La, made of common Yunjin, and at the city's heart a tower, Kunlun (昆仑).

The dome shielded everything beneath it.

Whatever lay outside it stayed outside.

Ten carriages carried the ten seeds.

The carriage of Bing (丙) went inside the dome, up to the crown of Kunlun.

The other nine rose high above Fusang and stopped there, far apart from one another, each in its fixed place, and poured their power down into Shangri-La.

A city of that size needs clerks as badly as it needs light.

Yao, which had refused to join Xihe, now offered to run it.

It sold Xihe its administration, and the price it asked was a place inside.

Xihe agreed.

Yao became its directorate, and Yao's elite moved through the gates into the lower tiers of the dome.

Higher up were the Xian (仙), the families who lived under the seeds.

The Xian looked down through the tiers at the newcomers and saw a lesser people.

Yao swallowed it, and kept its rooms.

That was the first layer of contempt in Shangri-La.

There would be others, and each would find someone beneath it.

## Years 600 to 2,800: Shiri, the Ten Suns

For two thousand two hundred years Fusang had ten suns, and it calls that age Shiri (十日).

Inside the dome it was paradise.

The light came through the dome filtered and mild, and under it the Xian edited their own genes until they no longer aged.

They still died, in the end, but the end came late and found them comfortable.

Outside it was the Scorching.

Nine suns hung over the land in their carriages and never moved, and the ground under them burned.

The people who worked out there were the Kuafu (夸父), and no one could survive that light in the body they were born with.

So the Kuafu went chrome.

They put metal into their bodies, and then more metal, until each of them was hard enough to last another shift.

Everything Shangri-La was made of lay outside the dome.

The Kuafu went out and mined it under nine burning suns, and they died by the millions, and the dome went on shining.

Some of what they dug out was Yang.

It killed the miners who touched it.

In Xihe's laboratories, where the dome filtered the light like everything else inside, the Yang did nothing at all.

Xihe judged it worthless and had it dumped in the Kuafu slag fields, under the open sky, where the raw light of nine suns lay on it every hour of every day.

Nobody from the dome stayed to watch.

Shiri also bred war beasts, to guard the mines.

They were made for the heat and the pits and the long watch over other people's metal.

They are still out there.

The heat is not.

## About year 2,780: Sheri, Shoot the Sun

Hou Yi grew up in the slag fields.

He was a Kuafu like any other, a man with metal in him, and he spent his youth among the things Xihe had thrown away.

The slag fields lay open to the sky, and the sky held nine suns.

So he saw what no laboratory in Shangri-La had ever seen: the Yang, lying in the raw light of the suns above, reacting to it.

He watched it until he understood what the antiseed was for.

The answer had been lying in the dump the whole time, in plain sight of the only people nobody asked.

Fusang calls what he began Sheri (射日), Shoot the Sun.

He could never test it on a real seed.

Everything he made, he made from the one thing he had seen, and from his faith that it would hold.

He needed money he did not have and cover he could not buy, and Yao gave him both, in secret.

With Yao's money and under Yao's cover, he forged ten arrows.

All the Yang on Fusang went into them, every grain of it.

To fire them he made a bow, the Red Bow, Tonggong (彤弓).

Ten arrows, for ten suns.

Fusang remembers him as the Archer.

He did not know what Yao wanted from them.

He knew what he wanted: the carriages out of the sky, and the Scorching over.

Yao promised him a price for the work: a place inside the dome, for him and for his people.

He had grown up in the slag, looking at the dome from the outside, and he believed the promise.

## Year 2,800: Yao's betrayal

Yao had never wanted the light gone.

It wanted the ten houses weakened, which is a different wish.

If all ten arrows flew, the light would go with them, and Yao's people would inherit the same frozen ruin as everyone else.

But if nine flew, and one sun stayed lit, and the tenth arrow stayed in Yao's hands, then Yao would stand second on Fusang, holding the one thing the first had reason to fear.

So on the eve of the shot, Yao stole the tenth arrow.

It hid the arrow outside the dome, in a hideout of its own in the deep ice, one that no one else knew of.

A beast lived there, a young one Yao had been raising for itself, and its name was Zaoya (凿牙).

Zaoya swallowed the arrow.

The Yang grew into its teeth.

In time they became chisel teeth, and what is in them still hungers for Yin.

Yao had kept its knife, and sheathed it in a living thing, and hidden the sheath in the cold, where no one was looking.

When the hour came, Hou Yi had nine arrows.

## Year 2,800: Shejiu, the Shooting of the Nine

The nine carriages hung far apart over Fusang.

Hou Yi knew that the moment one of them fell, Xihe would shield the rest, and he would never get a second shot at anything.

He had to take all nine almost at once.

Fusang calls it Shejiu (射九), the Shooting of the Nine.

He drew the Red Bow and loosed.

He drew again, and loosed again, nine times, faster than Xihe could understand what it was seeing.

With every shot the bow cracked a little further.

Fusang remembers Shejiu as a single moment, and it was one: nine shots, one breath.

He believed the arrows would bring the carriages down and nothing more.

He believed he was ending the Scorching.

Each arrow reached its seed, and each arrow and its seed consumed each other.

High above Fusang, nine suns went dark.

For the first time since the carriages rose, night fell on the slag fields.

The carriages broke where they hung, and nine burnt husks fell out of the sky.

The nine carriages are still there, broken, high above Fusang.

From each of the nine reactions a new seed was born, smaller and weaker than the sun it replaced, and it came down with the husks.

The Scorching ended, exactly as he had wanted.

Then Fusang began to freeze.

## Year 2,800: The fall of the houses

Nine houses woke under an empty sky.

Their power had hung above Fusang in carriages, and now it lay in pieces on the ground.

They kept their names and lost what the names had meant.

So they became the Nine Names, and went under Bing.

Today they hold one shared seat in government and vote as one bloc, never one by one: nine old voices that have learned to sound like one.

The ruins of the nine houses still lie out on the Ice.

Bing kept the last seed, inside the dome, at the crown of Kunlun, where no arrow had reached.

So Bing became Xihe, alone.

Today Xihe holds the seed, Kunlun, the Sanzuwu and most of the Yunjin, and it is the strongest force on Fusang.

The Riyu (日御), the Sun Charioteer, rules the house, with one carriage left to drive.

Yao, which had paid for the arrows and kept the last of them, stood exactly where it had planned to stand.

It became Xihe's ministry.

It was second on Fusang.

## Year 2,812: Yishuang, the Archer's Frost

Twelve years after the shot, Hou Yi came to the gates of the dome to claim his price.

Yao had promised a place inside, for him and for his people.

The gates stayed shut.

Behind the gates, under the filtered light, the last sun was still burning.

In front of them, the cold he had made was arriving.

He died in the first frozen winter.

He died knowing what he had done.

He had saved the Kuafu from fire, and he had condemned them to ice.

Both were true, and he could undo neither.

Fusang calls that winter Yishuang (羿霜), the Archer's Frost.

Every winter since has been one of its children.

## Years 2,812 to 2,850: Chang'e

His followers did not lie down in the ice beside him.

They took a name, ShenYi (神羿), and they took his bow.

The nine shots had cracked the Red Bow beyond repair, and it would never fire again.

So they kept it as what it had become, a relic of the Archer.

The freeze was chaos, and in the chaos ShenYi went looking for what had come down from the carriages.

They found the nine weak Yin seeds, and they kept them.

Xihe found only the husks.

Yao made sure of that.

Yao controlled the reports of the fall, and it buried them, so that Xihe came to believe Shejiu had left nothing behind but burnt frames.

Far out in the Ice, ShenYi built a dome of their own and set the nine weak seeds inside it to power it.

They cloaked it, so that it is invisible to the eye and to radar, and the cloak casts illusions.

The cold and the radiation of the Ice hide it further.

They named it after the moon goddess: Chang'e (嫦娥).

The power of the nine seeds also lifts Chang'e, and its signature reads like something in the air.

Yao heard the name and understood it, because Yao had funded Sheri and knew what had really come down from the sky.

So Yao told Xihe that Chang'e was something in orbit.

The lie fitted the name, and it fitted the readings, and it held.

From then on two lies held up the sky over Fusang: that the fall had left only husks, and that the moon was overhead.

Only the head of Yao knows that Chang'e is on the ground, and each head passes the secret to the next.

Chang'e does not stay where ShenYi built it.

It crawls across the land, and every few decades it moves.

Inside it ShenYi keep a cracked bow, nine small suns, and the memory of a man the gates shut out.

They know that Yao stole the tenth arrow.

They have never learned where it went.

They will never trust the house that betrayed the Archer, and a clan with no power has nothing to offer Yao, so the two have never allied.

## Years 2,850 to 3,200: The Sanzuwu

Xihe had nine burnt husks.

It spent three and a half centuries rebuilding them into machines, and called the machines the Sanzuwu.

The husks are only frames.

Nothing in them burns of its own accord: the last seed charges them, at the crown of Kunlun.

They are obedient, and they are ruthless.

Each walks on three legs, and each is built around circles and spheres.

There are nine, and they work in three triads of three.

Two triads are always out, and one is always home at Kunlun's crown, charging: six out, three home.

Each triad holds one machine to exploit the land, one to make war and one to scout, so every role is always covered.

The first triad is Yuhui (余晖), the tripod mining crawler; Yan (炎), the furnace-chested ground mech built for offence; and Rishi (日食), the eclipse disc, made for stealth.

The second is Rigui (日晷), the gnomon deep-driller; Heizi (黑子), the one-eyed walker that holds the line in defence; and Ri'er (日珥), the crescent gunship, built to be fast.

The third is Liming (黎明), the mobile refinery tower; Rimian (日冕), the corona flagship that commands; and Jin (烬), the ember sphere that tracks heat.

Every one of them carries a piece of a sun in its name.

Out on the Ice they strip the deep Yunjin craters, the same craters where Kuafu are sent as punishment.

The Archer put out nine suns.

Xihe gathered up what was left of them and taught it to walk.

## Year 3,200: Guanghuan, the Halo

Shangri-La needed workers.

So Xihe let some of the last seed's light out of the dome.

It spread that light over a ring of land around the dome, by day, and let the Kuafu live there.

Fusang calls the ring Guanghuan (光环), the Halo.

The name sounds like a blessing, and for part of every day it nearly is one.

Day in Guanghuan is a work shift.

The light comes out, and the Kuafu work the mines and refineries of the lit belt.

At night Xihe pulls the light back.

The cold falls, and the ice creeps back over the ground it lost that morning.

Guanghuan is a leak of light and warmth by day and a deadly cold every night, and it has been both for nearly seven thousand years.

Inside the dome the light never leaves.

Outside, it is lent for the length of a shift and taken back at nightfall.

## Years 3,300 to 4,000: The night builders

No one was coming to keep the Kuafu warm, so they learned to survive the night themselves.

They stole heat pipes from the dome and built over them, stacking home on home above the warmth, and those stacks became the Kang (炕) towers.

They lifted their homes off the killing ice on raised platforms held up by crude Yunjin lift, until whole streets hung above the frozen ground.

And they made the night bell, the warning that sounds before Xihe pulls the light back.

Every night since, it has been the last thing the Ring hears before the dark.

The platforms spread until there were ten thousand of them across the Ring, each one holding its crude lift against the cold.

Far out in the Ice, Chang'e rides on a refined version of the same craft.

Its signature hides in the noise of ten thousand Kuafu platforms, one more murmur among the poor.

## Years 3,500 to 4,800: The Pipe Wars

Heat is life in the Ring, and there has never been enough of it.

For thirteen centuries the Kuafu clans fought each other for the pipes and the towers above them.

A pipe was a night survived.

A tower was a clan that would see the morning.

Out of those wars rose the pipe kings, the Guanwang (管王): syndicates that rule the heat pipes and the Kang towers.

Xihe pays them by day and ShenYi pays them at night, and they have never seen a reason to refuse either.

The pipes all ran from the dome, so in the end every war in the Ring was a war over the dome's leftovers.

The Ring did not get warmer.

It got kings.

## Year 4,000: The first theft

In the year 4,000, ShenYi raided Shangri-La for the first time.

They came up through Tanggu (汤谷), the heat rift under the dome, past Jiuying (九婴), the nine-headed beast coiled there, on routes the pipe kings sold them.

That is still the way in: through Tanggu, past Jiuying, on routes the pipe kings sell, and through doors that Kuafu servants, and later the Ascended, open from the inside.

ShenYi took what they could carry, and they did not keep it.

They carried it out to the Ring and shared it.

They wanted then what they want now: the Kuafu free, and Xihe and Yao brought down.

To the Kuafu, ShenYi became the only people who had ever brought them warmth.

To the Xian, they became terrorists.

Both names stuck.

## Years 4,000 to 5,000: The Taming

The beasts that Shiri bred for the mines had outlasted Shiri.

For a thousand years Xihe captured them and rebuilt them as cyborgs.

The beasts need no Yin.

They take their power from what they eat, which is why they roam the Ice instead of coming home to Kunlun's crown like the Sanzuwu.

Yayu (猰貐) became a chrome hunting hound, and it tracks ShenYi for Xihe.

Jiuying lies coiled in Tanggu, nine heads in the rift, cooling the seed's heat for Xihe.

One beast went the other way.

Fengxi (封豨), the armoured tunnelling boar, serves ShenYi and digs their supply routes.

Two beasts still answer to no one.

Dafeng (大风) is a bird the size of a storm that rides the night winds.

Xiushe (修蛇) is a serpent that moves under the frozen sea.

Xihe and ShenYi have fought each other for thousands of years to tame them, and both beasts are still wild.

And there is one beast that no one fought over, because only Yao knew it mattered.

Zaoya has grown enormous since the Archer's time.

It sleeps for years in the deep ice, giving off no heat and no signal for the Sanzuwu to find, and the cold keeps it calm.

Now and then it wakes and roams, and when its path crosses Dafeng's or Xiushe's they clash.

Xihe has seen it, and thinks it is just another wild beast.

## Year 5,200: Heiyue, the Black Month

After a great theft, Xihe cancelled the day.

The night bell rang, the light went back into the dome, and for thirty days it did not come out.

Fusang calls that month Heiyue (黑月), the Black Month.

The ice did not creep that month.

It walked in, and it stayed.

When the light came back, a third of Guanghuan had frozen.

Inside the dome, the last sun had shone the whole time.

## Year 5,230: Huanluan, the Rising of the Ring

Thirty years after the Black Month, the Ring rose: Huanluan (环乱), the Rising of the Ring.

The Kuafu came down off their platforms and out of their towers and stormed the gates of the dome.

It lasted one night.

The Sanzuwu crushed it in that night, three legs at a time, and the gates held.

When the light came out over the Ring again, it came out on time, and the shift began.

## Year 5,240: The Ascension lottery

Ten years later, Yao found a better wall than the Sanzuwu.

Every year Yao lets a few Kuafu inside the dome, chosen by lottery.

Those who win become the Ascended: inside, and never equal.

Hope divides the Ring better than fear ever did.

A Kuafu who might win has a reason to keep quiet, and a reason to watch the neighbours.

Yao, which had once bargained for its own place inside, now keeps the door.

The ministry already held the law.

Now it held hope as well.

The Xian still look down on Yao as a lesser people, and Yao looks down on the Kuafu.

The Ascended learn quickly where that leaves them.

Each layer of Fusang has someone beneath it, and each layer holds on to that someone with both hands.

## Years 5,500 to 7,000: The Faith Wars

The Archer split the Ring in two.

On one side stood the Gongmen (弓门), the Bow Sect, who worship the Archer, and among whom ShenYi find their recruits.

On the other stood the Xinmin (信民), the Faithful, who believe that obedience earns Ascension, who blame the Archer for the ice, and who inform on the Bow Sect.

For centuries they burned each other's towers.

Xihe hardly had to speak.

Its propaganda says: the Archer froze your grandparents.

It is true.

That is why it works, and why the Ring has never fully sided with ShenYi.

Both sects are right about him, which is why neither could win.

## Year 6,000: The Icewalkers

Some Kuafu gave up on the Ring and walked into the Ice.

They became nomads, the Icewalkers, the Bingmin (冰民).

They walked out past the last platform, among the ruins of the nine houses, and did not come back at the bell.

The Bingmin are augmented, like every Kuafu, and they live by mining Yunjin and hunting beasts.

And they carry ShenYi's supplies.

What reaches Chang'e comes through Fengxi's tunnels or on an Icewalker's back.

The Icewalkers traded the night bell for the open cold, and the shift for the hunt.

## About year 6,500: The first fading

Chang'e's scientists found that its power was falling.

Very slowly, year by year, the light of the nine weak seeds was fading.

They built Yutu (玉兔), a battery that can recharge Chang'e.

It has one condition: only a living seed can charge it.

Yutu is ShenYi's real reason to reach the seed at Kunlun's crown.

Charging it would keep Chang'e alive without firing an arrow at all.

## Year 6,800: Shanguang, the Flicker

ShenYi smuggled the first Yutu up to the crown of Kunlun and drew from the seed.

For one hour, the last sun dimmed.

That hour is Shanguang (闪光), the Flicker.

The Xian looked up at their one sun and watched it falter, and an hour later it was whole again.

Nobody inside the dome has ever learned why.

Since then every faction on Fusang has studied the seed, convinced it is dying.

Each later Yutu run is rare, once in many generations, and each one becomes a ShenYi legend.

The great fear of Fusang, that its sun is dying, began as a theft.

## Year 7,500: The Long Theft

ShenYi began stealing from Shangri-La every day.

They take parts and cells, whatever keeps Chang'e running between Yutu runs.

The Ring calls the stolen light cells ember cells, and an ember cell is worth killing for.

In Kunlun, Xihe's scientists have measured the last seed for thousands of years, and its power has not dropped at all.

The Busi (不死), the Deathless, were the ones who dared to say what that meant.

They are the few among the Xian who went past agelessness into true immortality, through their research into the seeds.

Their conclusion is that the ten original seeds shine forever.

Xihe keeps it hidden.

Eternal does not mean safe.

It means only that the seed never runs down on its own: the Yang can still consume it, and Xihe fears the tenth arrow as much as it ever did.

The Busi fear it most of all, because their immortality is tied to the last seed, and so they guard its secret hardest.

Xihe keeps the fear alive on purpose.

It tells the Kuafu that their labour is what keeps the seed burning, so that every shift in the mines feels like keeping the sun alive.

Xihe rations even the charging of the Sanzuwu and argues over it, because the public still believes the seed is dying.

## Year 9,000: The Daily Hunt

By the year 9,000, hunting ShenYi had become a daily institution.

Yao runs it, as it runs the law and the lottery.

There are patrols and bounties, and there are executions, shown on the dome.

The hunt catches ShenYi one by one.

It never finds a hideout.

Part of the reason is the shifts.

Many ShenYi work the day shift like any Kuafu, in the same mines, under the same borrowed light, and that is how they hide.

The other part of the reason is Yao.

A living ShenYi keeps Yao needed.

Someone has to run the hunts, and someone has to be the threat that makes Yao's arrow matter.

So Yao's hunts take the expendable and miss the core.

Every execution shown on the dome is real, and none of them reaches the core.

The faces on the dome are the faces of the shift, and after the execution the shift goes back to work.

## Year 10,000: Yiwan, the Ten Thousandth

Ten thousand years after the star died, Xihe found a ShenYi hideout.

It was the first time Xihe had ever found one.

Fusang calls the year Yiwan (一万), the Ten Thousandth.

Xihe learned that Chang'e is on the ground, and it found clues to where.

For seven thousand years Xihe had searched the sky for Chang'e.

Now, for the first time, it looks at the ground.

And it has begun to wonder who told it to look up.

Xihe runs this campaign itself, without Yao.

The Sanzuwu find one ShenYi hideout after another and burn it, and the Ring watches the smoke.

It watches from the platforms and the Kang towers, between shifts, with the night bell still to come.

The Sanzuwu are the husks of the nine suns, and the light that drives them comes from the tenth.

Now they walk the Ice and the Ring on three legs, burning the hiding places of the Archer's heirs, as if the suns he shot had come back to finish the argument.

ShenYi hold almost no power: no whole seed, no arrow, a broken bow and a fading moon.

What they have is fighters and the skill to hide.

That skill has kept them alive for seven thousand years.

Even now, Xihe has not moved against Yao.

Xihe presses Yao at every turn, and Yao would turn on Xihe if it ever could.

Still, Xihe tolerates Yao.

Only Yao knows where the tenth arrow is.

And Yao keeps a dead man's switch: a sleeper agent, whose name no one knows, under orders to wake Zaoya if Yao falls.

The tenth arrow is the only thing left that can break the last seed, and Zaoya is the arrow now.

Woken and unsteered, Zaoya goes for the seed.

Its bite would consume the seed and leave a husk and one more weak seed, and Shangri-La would lose its eternal sun.

Somewhere in the deep ice it sleeps.

Whether the sweep of Yiwan has passed near it, and how near, this chronicle does not say.

The hiding is over.

The old count ends here, in the smoke.

The story begins here.

## What the chronicle knows that Fusang does not

The sun at the crown of Kunlun is not dying.

A seed that fell in the Sky Fire never runs down on its own.

The Busi know that much, and Xihe has buried their conclusion under a fear it feeds every day.

The hour the sun went dim was not the sun failing.

It was ShenYi, drinking from it with Yutu.

The tenth arrow lies in no vault.

It lives in Zaoya's teeth, asleep in the cold.

Zaoya is Yao's, raised since the Archer's time.

Xihe has looked straight at it and seen only a beast.

Yao's hunts were never meant to win.
